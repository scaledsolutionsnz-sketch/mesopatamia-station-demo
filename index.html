Welcome

Situated right in the centre of the South Island, surrounded by the majestic Two Thumb range and the huge braided Rangitata River.

Mesopotamia is a working merino sheep, deer and cattle station in the high-country headwaters of the Rangitata gorge — just over 72,000 acres rising from around 457m (1,800ft) on the valley floor to 2,660m (7,500ft) at the tops.

Alongside the farm, we welcome guests for hunting, accommodation and a whole range of high-country experiences. It's a small, unspoiled piece of South Island paradise, run by the Prouting family since 1945.

A unique free-range hunting experience

Other activities

More to do in the high country

From the river to the tops, there's an adventure for every guest.

Fishing

The Rangitata and its tributaries offer excellent salmon and trout fishing. Fish independently or arrange a guided flyout to remote tarns with a picnic.

Horse Trekking

Bring your own horses for valley rides or braided-river treks. Advanced riders can stay overnight at mustering huts with camp cooking and fire-warmed bathing.

Heli-Tours

The station has its own MD NOTAR helicopter — perfect for a bird's-eye view of the breathtaking scenery, aerial sightseeing and drop-off hiking.

Hiking

Plan a multi-day trip into some of New Zealand's most spectacular and remote country, staying in one of our back-country mustering huts.

Mountain Biking

From day trips up the Rangitata valley to extended backcountry adventures — heli-biking options available too.

Rafting & Kayaking

Enjoy a day trip with the famous Rangitata Rafts, or kayak the river. Winter heli-skiing and heli-boarding in the Two Thumb range on request.

History

The land between two rivers

"Mesopotamia" comes from the Middle East and means "the land between two rivers" — a name given by the English writer Samuel Butler in 1860, because the station sits between the Rangitata and Forest Creek.

Butler, famous for his satire Erewhon, first occupied a small 'V' hut at Forest Creek before moving to the present homestead site two months later, establishing the station in 1860.

"I went up my mountain in a fog... I fancy that I can see the downs, the huts, the plain and the river bed — the distant roar of water, oh wonderful!"

Later runholders included the Campbell brothers and George McMillan. Malcolm Prouting bought Mesopotamia in 1945, and the Prouting family farm it to this day. Guests can explore the original homestead remains, Samuel Butler memorabilia, and walk to the original 'V' hut site.

Getting here

Finding Mesopotamia

By roadTake your time up the Rangitata gorge road, perhaps stopping to picnic at the Te Wanahu flats at Peel Forest. Christchurch is 2.5 hours; Geraldine, the nearest town, is 1 hour.
By airAll planes and helicopters are welcome. The airstrip is a 1km flat grass strip with tie-down spots; avgas available if pre-arranged.
By 4WD tourLocal operators can take you directly to Mesopotamia, or build it into one of their high-country tours.

Airstrip coordinates: S 43° 38' 44" E 170° 54' 02"
