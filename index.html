Our Story

Where History Meets
The High Country

Mesopotamia Station sits in the upper Rangitata River valley in Canterbury — one of New Zealand's most storied high country properties. Named after the ancient land between rivers, it's a place where dramatic mountain landscapes meet generations of pastoral farming tradition.

From the station's origins in the 1860s to today, Mesopotamia has remained a working high country farm — raising stock on tussock and snowgrass runs that reach into the Southern Alps.

Life on the Station

High Country Farming

Running sheep and cattle across vast tussock runs at the foot of the Southern Alps — farming the way it's been done for generations in Canterbury's back country.

Iconic Landscapes

Mesopotamia sits in some of New Zealand's most spectacular scenery — mountain ranges, braided rivers, and sweeping high country runs that stretch to the horizon.

🏡

Heritage Station

A working property steeped in New Zealand history, sitting in the valley once farmed by Samuel Butler — the landscape that inspired his writing and captured his imagination.

🌾

Canterbury Back Country

Pure, remote, and expansive — the station sits beyond the Rangitata Gorge in a valley that feels entirely separate from the modern world.
