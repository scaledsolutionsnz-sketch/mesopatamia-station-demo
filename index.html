Our Story

A Living Piece of
New Zealand History

Named by Samuel Butler in 1860 — the same year he first ran sheep on this land — Mesopotamia Station sits at the headwaters of the Rangitata Gorge, nestled between the soaring peaks of the Two Thumbs Range and the Southern Alps. The name itself, meaning "land between two rivers," perfectly describes its dramatic setting between the Rangitata and Forest Creek.

Today, the station runs 11,000 merino sheep, 2,200 deer, and 500 cattle across one of the most spectacular landscapes in the country — a fully working farm that also welcomes guests to experience the raw beauty and adventure of New Zealand's high country, exactly as generations before have done.

Whether you're a trophy hunter, an adventurer, or simply looking for an escape unlike anywhere else in New Zealand, Mesopotamia offers something you'll carry with you long after you leave.

High Country Accommodation
